# 12. Peer Psychology & Healthy Competition

You study among people. Groups create pressure, comparison, and norms. Understand them so you don’t get played by the crowd.

## Common classroom patterns

| Pattern | Looks like | Risk |
|---------|------------|------|
| Effort hiding | “I barely studied” (often false) | You under-prepare to look cool |
| Grade theatre | Loud panic or loud bragging | Anxiety or ego, not learning |
| Crab bucket | Pulling ambitious friends down | Guilt for trying |
| Cram culture | All-nighters romanticised | Sleep loss |
| Gatekeeping | Hiding tips | Wasted time |
| Busyness status | “I’m so overloaded” as identity | Stress mistaken for success |

## Peer types

1. **Allies** — honest effort, share methods  
2. **Respectful competitors** — raise the bar, stay fair  
3. **Distractors** — fun, high noise  
4. **Energy drains** — endless complaint  
5. **Toxic comparers** — weaponise ranks  

Be polite to all. Build deep study ties with **1–2**.

## Healthy competition

Compete with **your last score** and the **syllabus**. Use peers as fuel, not enemies.

**Do:** compare methods; treat ranks as signals; celebrate others; keep some goals private if needed.  
**Don’t:** need others to fail; cheat; let one person’s pace own your life; live in anxiety group chats.

### After you compare (3 steps)

1. Name one thing they do well  
2. Steal the method  
3. Schedule one action in 24 hours  

If comparison only creates shame, **reduce exposure**.

## Controllable scoreboard

```text
This week vs last week
- Timed papers done:
- Error types reduced:
- Sleep nights on target:
- Deep blocks kept:
- Drama hours (aim down):
```

Winning the hallway game is optional. Winning the **skills game** is not.
