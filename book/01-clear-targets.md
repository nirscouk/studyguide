# 1. Set Clear Targets First

Vague goals produce vague effort. High marks start with **concrete outcomes**.

## Outcomes over subjects

| Weak goal | Strong goal |
|-----------|-------------|
| “Study maths” | “Understand and solve quadratic equations confidently” |
| “Do biology” | “Explain mitosis vs meiosis and answer 5 exam-style questions without notes” |
| “Revise history” | “Write a timed 15-minute essay plan on Topic X using three named sources” |

## The 80/20 rule for exams

Identify the **20% of topics/skills that produce ~80% of exam marks** and prioritise those.

- Syllabus weightings and past papers reveal what examiners actually test  
- High-frequency question types deserve more practice than obscure edge cases  
- Master the core, then expand outward  

## Weekly goals that stick

Make goals **specific and measurable**:

- “Complete and self-mark **two past papers** on Topic X by Friday”  
- “Active-recall **Chapter 4** until I can explain every section from a blank page”  
- “Error-log **all mistakes** from this week’s practice and re-test them Sunday”  

### Checklist

- [ ] Every subject has 3–5 concrete outcomes this term  
- [ ] Top mark-weighted topics are listed first  
- [ ] This week has at least one measurable deliverable (paper, summary, or timed set)
